## The whole lecture in one paragraph

> Every VLM is **a vision encoder (almost always a ViT) + a connector + a consumer**. The models differ in the connector and what the consumer does. **CLIP** has no connector beyond two projection matrices. It trains an image tower and a text tower so matching pairs land close together in one shared space (symmetric InfoNCE over an N×N similarity matrix). That gives zero-shot classification but no language output. **LLaVA** takes CLIP's frozen ViT, adds a tiny linear or MLP **projector** that turns patch features into "foreign-language word embeddings", and lets a chat LLM do the reasoning. It is trained in two stages (align the projector, then instruction-tune) on 158K examples that text-only GPT-4 wrote from COCO captions and boxes. **Qwen-VL** swaps the projector for a **cross-attention adapter with 256 learned queries and 2D positions**, so any resolution becomes a fixed 256-token budget. It also teaches the LLM to **write bounding boxes as text** (`<ref>…</ref><box>(x1,y1),(x2,y2)</box>`), which gives it grounding, OCR and multi-image reasoning. **SAM** leaves language out entirely. A heavy image encoder runs once, a ~50 ms decoder turns each click or box into three candidate masks, and a model-in-the-loop data engine produced 1.1B masks. In real systems they are **composed**: CLIP retrieves, Qwen-VL grounds, SAM segments.
